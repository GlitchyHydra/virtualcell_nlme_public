## The predictors (rungs)

Every source line gives, per (target, gene), an effect `beta = log2(mean_pert / mean_ctrl)` and a standard error `s` (Stage 1). A rung turns these into one predicted effect per (target, gene) for a new line (Stage 2, `eda/stage2.py`).

| Rung | What it predicts | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | The mean effect of each gene over all targets and lines (genes seen in fewer than 30 estimates: 0) | "Every knockdown moves the same genes a bit." No target information; the floor for pooling |
| v2 | Inverse-variance fixed-effect average over the lines that measured the pair; falls back to v1 if none did | Adds target-specific information; assumes all lines share one true effect |
| v3 | Random-effects average (weights 1/(s² + tau²), tau² = between-line variance per expression bin); falls back to v1 | Like v2 but allows lines to differ, so no single line dominates. Predictive sd = sqrt(var + tau²) |
| v3b | v3 followed by a sparse prior (point mass at 0 plus zero-centred normals, fitted by EM); prediction = posterior mean | Shrinks weak, noisy effects to about zero; also gives P(null) and lfsr for responder selection |

### Why v3 beat v3b although the proxy preferred v3b
- The proxy asked "is the effect estimate close to the truth?" and shrinkage helps there: it removes noise from thousands of weak estimates.
- The real scorer calls DE genes on the generated cells and compares them with the real DE calls (fid, reach, jac) and ranks whole profiles by cosine (pds). Those metrics need the weak but real effects to be present. The prior put about 99.5% of its weight on "no effect" and pushed them to about zero, so the generated cells look like unchanged controls (E3 sits close to R0 on fid and jac).
- The same shrinkage lowers raw `mse` (1.73 against 3.23), so v3b reduces profile noise but loses more on the other metrics than it gains. v3 wins overall through `pds`, yet its profiles are too far from the real ones: effects are probably too large and too noisy where the data are thin.

## What was tried, in order (proxy metrics only, before the scorer was wired in)

1. **Beta by hand instead of a negative-binomial model (Stage 1).** `beta = log2(mean_pert / mean_ctrl)` on CPM with a 1 CPM pseudocount and a delta-method `s`. Reason: the sources arrive in different forms (K562 pseudobulk means, CD4 precomputed DESeq2 log-FC and SE, X-Atlas and H1 single cells), and one transparent formula puts them all in one table.
2. **Two-stage pooling (Stage 2).** Rungs v1 to v3b above; checked on the worked examples in `docs/archive/MODEL_DESIGN.md`.
3. **Two lines (K562 + CD4+ T Rest) against H1.** Rank correlation 0.02 to 0.04, sign accuracy 0.52 to 0.54: signal only where the sources see the target strongly; two lines are not enough. CD4+ T Stim conditions added nothing (same donors, not independent lines).
4. **More lines because pooling needs them: HCT116 (110 files) and HEK293T (224 files) from X-Atlas, plus H1.** With four lines the best proxy rung reached rank correlation 0.0365 and sign accuracy 0.551 (v1: 0.020 and 0.509).
5. **Silent genes broke v1.** `z_sd` was 8.8 for HCT116: genes seen in one estimate got that value as their mean with spread 0. Fixed with a 30-estimate minimum in `generic()`. `s` itself was never the problem.
6. **Scoring only well-expressed genes, and dropping low-CPM genes from training.** Higher CPM raises every score; dropping from training changes nothing. Kept all genes.
7. **Add-a-line test (hold out HCT116 or HEK293T, with and without H1).** H1 neutral, so it stays in the final fit.
8. **Cell generator v0 and the real scorer** (R0 to E3): resample 400 control cells per target; thin counts binomially for decreases, add Poisson counts for increases (`eda/15_generate_h1.py`).

Challenge FAQ (pasted 2026-10-19): "you may use any data to improve the performance of your predictions" provided you have the appropriate permissions. So external data is allowed; what remains open is whether non-commercial licence terms count as permission for a prize-eligible entry. KOLF2.1J (CC BY 4.0) is the licence-clean replacement for HCT116 and HEK293T if needed.
- H1 effects are tiny (median |beta| 0.04), so the H1 numbers may understate what the larger-effect panel allows; only the A/B/C leaderboard settles that.
- **How mse is really scored (read 2026-10-20 in `cell_eval2/metrics/delta.py`).** expr_mse_unbiased_capped_norm = sum over
  targets of (||pred bulk - real bulk||^2 - C_pred - C_real) / sum of (||real - control||^2 - C_real - C_ctrl), in
  log1p(5e4-normalised) pseudobulk space, own target gene left out, ratio of sums. C is each side's sampling correction (a
  delete-1 cell jackknife of the pseudobulk). The prediction's C is capped at the real side's (#247) and, as a total, at the
  submission's own across-target spread (#348). For honest i.i.d. cells our C matches our pseudobulk noise, so noise costs
  nothing in expectation and only signal error counts: that is why our proxy reproduces the leaderboard (H34). A submission
  that pins each pseudobulk exactly while keeping per-cell scatter collects up to C_real per target without paying it. The
  organisers call this an exploit ("pin the per-(p, g) sums"; measured worth 0.72-0.90 of the metric's range; closed for flat
  submissions by #348, explicitly left open for submissions with large across-target spread). The public entry above pins its
  bulks exactly (`repair_bulk_totals`). Rank ~245 teams' raw mse of 0.82-0.89 may come from this rather than from biology.
  **Not adopted**: it scores the noise correction, not the prediction; ask the organisers before any emission that is not
  i.i.d. Size of the allowance from our Stage 1 tables (real-side sampling noise at 400 cells / noise-corrected effect energy,
  same weights as the mse proxy): K562 0.36, HCT116 0.23, HEK293T 0.33, H1 0.19 (CD4 not usable: its s is a donor-level
  DESeq2 SE). A pinned submission with no skill would read raw 0.64-0.81, the band where rank ~245 teams sit (0.82-0.89); no
  honest transfer of public data gets below 0.98 (H34).
